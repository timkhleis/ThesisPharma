{\Large\bfseries First Results: Acquisitions and Inventor Productivity}\hfill
{\small 29 June 2026}

{\small Preliminary descriptive evidence for supervisor discussion. The causal estimation panel is complete, but no CS(2021) coefficient is reported until the official \texttt{did} package runs successfully.}

\vspace{2pt}
\resultbox{%
\textbf{Three preliminary facts.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The corrected sample is anchored to the published 513-merger universe. It retains 478 uniquely matched target-spine deals after Cassi's target-side exclusions; 345 lie in the 1993--2010 clean window.
  \item Among 28,279 pre-deal target inventors in the clean sample, mean annual patenting falls from 0.474 in year $-1$ to 0.246 in the deal year and 0.113 by year $+5$. These are descriptive means, not treatment effects.
  \item The late decline is concentrated on the extensive margin. Continuing-patenter stayers remain productive, while the broader persistent-stayer sample increasingly includes silent inventors and career exits.
\end{itemize}}

\vspace{5pt}
\textbf{Why the deal count changed}

The group-history files cover more mergers than the published sample. The previous reconstruction inadvertently admitted those additional events. The corrected code first anchors each event to \texttt{deal\_map.deal\_id}, excludes 22 unmatched deals and one ambiguous match, and then applies Cassi's drop flags.

\textbf{Interpretation discipline}

\small
\begin{itemize}
  \item The main full-cohort design is fixed before treatment and does not condition on staying.
  \item \texttt{T\_Ever\_Stayed} records at least one acquirer-affiliated patent within five years; it does not imply continuous retention.
  \item Persistent-stayer and continuing-patenter profiles describe selected post-treatment populations.
  \item The divergence between the two panels shows why career visibility must not be silently imposed as a sample restriction.
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
